About

I read the evidence, then write the part people can actually use.

I'm pursuing my M.Pharm in Pharmacy Practice at the Raghavendra Institute of Pharmaceutical Education and Research (RIPER), Anantapur — an autonomous campus, meaning the college designs and examines its own curriculum — and I live in Koppal, Karnataka.

My work is driven by one question: how can healthcare knowledge be communicated, organised and translated into understanding that helps people make better decisions? During the move from B.Pharm to M.Pharm I became increasingly interested in a problem that sits beyond pharmacy itself: healthcare rarely suffers from a lack of information — it suffers from the gap between information and understanding.

I currently build PMAS — an offline-first, five-language telepharmacy platform for rural and remote chronic care, with a patient app, a pharmacist portal and a privacy model that keeps identifiable patient records separate from de-identified clinical data.

Before that I built TechStudyHubCore, a solo learning ecosystem for pharmacy, nursing and school-level students. It is now archived: alongside M.Pharm and PMAS it became too broad for one person to maintain responsibly, so I preserved the engineering record rather than let it decay.

I don't see technology as a replacement for healthcare professionals. I see it as a tool for improving how knowledge is communicated, understood and applied — through visual frameworks, structured learning pathways and AI-assisted systems.

As I progress through my M.Pharm, I want to investigate these questions through research, practical projects and collaboration with clinicians, educators and health-technology professionals.

This site is a public thinking journal as much as a portfolio — what I'm learning about pharmacy practice, healthcare communication, digital health and the systems that connect knowledge with better outcomes.
